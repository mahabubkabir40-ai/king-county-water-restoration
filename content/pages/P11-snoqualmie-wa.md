## Common Causes of Water Damage in Snoqualmie

- **Valley floor flooding and groundwater (Historic Downtown & Meadowbrook).** Above Snoqualmie Falls, the river winds right through town. Heavy rain and mountain snowmelt push groundwater into crawl spaces and lower levels even when water doesn't reach the doorstep.
- **Upstairs plumbing and appliance leaks (Snoqualmie Ridge).** Two-story homes built since the late 1990s often have second-floor laundry rooms and master baths where a failed washing machine hose or toilet supply line runs down through first-floor ceilings and hardwood floors.
- **East-wind freezes (98065 & 98068).** Cold air pouring down the I-90 corridor freezes pipes in garages, exterior walls, and crawl spaces, especially in unoccupied cabins at Snoqualmie Pass (`98068`).
- **Windstorm power outages.** When sump pumps lose power during a storm, crawl spaces on the valley floor and hillside benches fill up fast.

## Snoqualmie Tip: Two-Story Leaks Travel Farther Than They Look

In Snoqualmie Ridge homes, water from a second-floor laundry room or bathroom rarely stays in one room. It runs along floor joists, drops inside interior wall cavities, and spreads under first-floor hardwood or laminate planks. Even if you mopped up the surface water, ask the technician to scan the ceiling below and the surrounding walls with a moisture meter.
