## Common Causes of Water Damage in Carnation

- **Two rivers, one floodplain.** When the Tolt and the Snoqualmie run high together, groundwater rises under homes in and around town and fills crawl spaces, even where the river stays in its banks.
- **Septic systems in the wet season.** Outside town, many homes rely on septic. Saturated winter drainfields are a common cause of backups into the house.
- **Wells and pressure tanks.** Rural homes have extra plumbing, such as pressure tanks, well lines, and pump houses, that can freeze or fail.
- **Pipe freezes.** The open valley gets cold during arctic outbreaks, and crawl space pipes take the hit.

## Carnation Tip: Document Where the Water Came From

After a storm, water in a Carnation home can come from more than one place at once: rising groundwater, a backed-up septic system, or a pipe that froze in the same cold snap. That matters for insurance. Plumbing failures are often covered by homeowners policies, while rising floodwater usually needs flood insurance. Photograph the source of the water and tell the restoration company what you saw so the cause is documented correctly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**My septic backed up into the house. Who do I call first?**
Stop using water in the house. Call a septic service to pump the tank and check the system, and a restoration company to clean and disinfect the inside of the home. Both are usually needed.

**Can someone reach Carnation during a flood?**
Access can be limited when valley roads close. The company you're connected with will tell you what's possible. It's another reason to call early.

**Do I need flood insurance if I'm not right on the river?**
Groundwater and flooding can affect homes outside the most obvious flood zones. Talk to your agent. Flood policies through the National Flood Insurance Program generally have a 30-day waiting period, so don't wait for the forecast.
