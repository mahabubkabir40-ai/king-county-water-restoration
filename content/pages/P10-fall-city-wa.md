## Common Causes of Water Damage in Fall City

- **A fast-rising river.** The Raging River can come up quickly when heavy rain hits the hills. Homes near the river and the Snoqualmie see groundwater in crawl spaces during big storms.
- **Wells and septic.** Most rural properties here have private wells and septic systems. Well lines, pressure tanks, and saturated drainfields all cause their own kind of water damage.
- **Hillside drainage.** Homes on the slopes toward Lake Alice and Mitchell Hill can get water against the foundation when yard drains clog.
- **Winter freezes.** Rural homes with long supply lines and outbuildings are exposed when temperatures drop.

## Fall City Tip: On a Well? Shut Off the Pump

If a pipe bursts in a home on a private well, closing a valve isn't always enough. The well pump can keep running and pushing water through the break. Turn off the breaker for the well pump as well as closing the main valve. Once the leak is fixed and the power is back on, check the crawl space for any other splits as the system repressurizes.

**Fall City is unincorporated. Does that change anything?**
Not for getting help. Drying and cleanup start the same way anywhere. If the repairs afterward involve structural, plumbing, or electrical work, permits would go through King County rather than a city.

**Is flood damage from the Raging River covered?**
Usually not under a standard homeowners policy. Damage from rising water typically requires flood insurance. A burst pipe is a different claim and is often covered.
